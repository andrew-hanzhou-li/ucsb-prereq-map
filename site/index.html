      About this site

      Pick a UCSB major or minor and see every course in it on one page, in prerequisite order, with what each course needs and what it leads to.

      How to read the map

      • Steps. The required courses, and the courses from other departments they need, are in rows called steps. A course's prerequisites are in earlier steps. Steps are an order, not a schedule, so they work whether you started as a freshman, transferred or switched majors.
      • Electives are listed under the steps, one block per elective list, with the rule for that list (for example "complete at least 6").
      • Point at or click a course. Amber arrows lead to it from everything it needs, all the way back. Blue arrows lead from it to what it unlocks. Everything else fades. Clicking also opens the course's details: prerequisites in full and in the catalog's own words.
      • Getting back: press Esc or the panel's Close button to go back to the whole map. Back in the panel returns to the course you looked at before. "All majors and minors" returns to the list.
      • Mark courses as taken and the ones you can take next get a green outline.

      The colors

      • Required
      • Required, but you pick one of a few
      • Elective
      • From another department, needed first
      • Listed in the catalog but not offered now
      • You've marked it taken
      • You can take it next

      Your marks stay on your device

      Courses you mark as taken are saved in your browser on this device only. They're never sent anywhere, and the site has no accounts and no analytics. Clearing your browser's data, or using a private window, removes the marks.

      Sharing and saving

      The address bar names the program and the course you've selected, so you can copy it and send it. The site is also available as one file that opens in any browser, even offline.

      Where the data comes from

      Everything comes from the UCSB General Catalog: the course list, each course's prerequisites, and each program's requirements. Course prerequisites are written in plain English in the catalog, so a program reads them into "all of" and "one of" rules. Program requirements are copied from the catalog's structured lists.

      What it can't check

      • Grades. "C or better" is shown, but marking a course taken assumes you met the grade.
      • Conditions like class standing, major restrictions or instructor approval are listed, not checked. ECE 139, for example, has no course prerequisites in the catalog, only "open to EE and CE majors", so it sits with the other upper-division core courses.
      • Rules written as notes (for example "a maximum of 4 units of 199 courses") appear as text in the panel.
      • Some programs have no map. The catalog doesn't list course requirements for College of Creative Studies majors, the individual major or Undeclared.
      • Unclear wording. When the catalog mixes "and" and "or" without punctuation, the panel says the reading is a best guess and shows the original wording.

      An unofficial student project

      It isn't made or endorsed by UC Santa Barbara. Requirements change, and the catalog is the official source. Confirm your plan with your department advisor.
